# Disclaimer

rentsmart produces an informational second opinion on what a home might rent for.
Read this before relying on anything it prints.

## What a brief is not

- **Not an appraisal.** No one inspected the home. Condition, finishes, upgrades,
  layout, views, noise, and lease terms are invisible to it unless you supply them.
- **Not financial, legal, or tax advice.** Pricing a rental, screening tenants,
  drafting a lease, and filing taxes have legal and financial consequences. Talk to
  a licensed professional for those decisions.
- **Not a guarantee.** A rent range is an estimate with a stated basis and stated
  limitations. Homes rent above and below it.

## What the numbers are built from

- **Asking prices, not signed leases.** Rental comps come from listings. What a
  tenant finally signs can differ. Every comp is labeled `asking` or `leased`, and
  the rent estimate states which basis it uses.
- **One data vendor.** All data comes from the RentCast API, which can be
  incomplete, delayed, or wrong. rentsmart shows each value's source and date so you
  can judge it, but it does not verify the vendor's records.
- **No MLS.** Your agent may see listings and closed leases that rentsmart cannot.
- **No property-level day predictions.** Market speed is zip-level. Pricing
  scenarios describe direction ("likely faster than the zip median"), never a
  number of days for your home.
- **Advice is rule-based.** Suggestions and questions come from fixed rules that cite
  facts already in the brief. If the evidence isn't there, no advice is given; the
  absence of advice is not a sign that everything is fine.

## Fair housing and privacy

- rentsmart uses property facts and transactions only. It does not use or report
  neighborhood quality judgments, demographics, or crime data.
- It does not read or show owner names, contact details, or other personal data,
  even when the vendor provides them.
- Cached responses stay on your machine (`~/.cache/rentsmart/`).

## Your responsibility

You decide what to ask, whom to rent to, and on what terms, and you are responsible
for complying with fair housing and landlord-tenant law where the property is.
